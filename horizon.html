Scenarios

Where you stand today

Cash flow & saving

Income, outgoings, rent, and where the difference goes — the whole chain in one place. Every pound left over flows automatically into the growth pot below — no manual allocation, nothing to decide each month. The emergency pot is separate and protected: never touched by any scenario, never counted as spendable.

Emergency pot

Protected — you manage this yourself. Never grown, never drawn on, never counted toward net worth in any projection.

Buckets

Where the surplus above actually goes. Each bucket gets its own explicit £/mo — a Holiday fund, a House/repairs fund, a Family/emergency fund, a Retirement fund — and that figure is exactly what happens, nothing automatic on top. Mark each one Grows indefinitely (the deposit and retirement projections are driven entirely by this bucket's own £/mo — never spent on purpose, just compounds) or Used regularly (a sinking fund — shows what your wage is going toward and what that adds up to annually, tracked honestly, but completely excluded from every deposit and retirement calculation). Anything left over after every bucket's own allocation is shown, but goes nowhere on its own — raise a bucket's allocation if you want it put to work.

One-off costs

Timed, lumpy costs a monthly figure can't capture — a boiler, a car, a wedding. Each is entered in today's money and draws from a pot you nominate (deposit, savings, or pension) at the age it happens, or repeats every N years. Shown as markers on every scenario's path chart (block 3) and factored into Through retirement (block 5).

Simplifications for v1: purchase costs are a flat estimate rather than banded stamp duty; growth and amortisation are calculated annually rather than monthly; no tax, ISA/pension allowance, or income-tax modelling. Every figure is calculated in nominal (future) pounds internally — that's the only way compounding stays correct — and only converted to today's money at display time when "Real" is selected above, using the inflation rate below. Enough to compare shapes of futures, not a substitute for a mortgage advisor, pensions administrator, or independent financial advice.

Checkpoints

Save a dated snapshot of where things stand — income, outgoings, buckets, one-off costs, pensions, and progress on both scenarios — so you can look back later and see what's actually changed. Comparing two checkpoints against each other comes in a future release; for now, save and review.

Year by year tracker

One entry per age — record each bucket's actual balance as the year goes, and see it checked against what the model projected for this age. Growth buckets get a real comparison (they behave identically regardless of buy/rent, so the model's own trajectory is the reference); sinking funds don't — they're spent irregularly by design, so there's nothing to check them against, just a record of where things stood and why.

Horizon is a scenario-comparison tool for household planning conversations, not financial advice. Projections depend entirely on the assumptions you set above and are illustrative, not a forecast or a recommendation to buy, rent, or invest in any particular way. Pension figures are estimates built from what you enter — check accrued defined benefit amounts against your latest annual benefit statement, and your own State Pension forecast at gov.uk, rather than relying on this app's defaults.
