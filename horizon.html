Sensitivity — what if saving stops early

Saving realistically reduces or stops before retirement. This shows how much the age-100 figures and sustainability depend on Growth Pot saving actually continuing all the way to target retirement age.

Net worth over time

HZN-105: contributions stop at your target retirement age — from then on, the pots only move from investment growth (plus house price growth, once bought), pension income, and what outgoings + one-off costs actually cost. A flattening or decline afterwards is expected, not a bug. HZN-108: DC run-out is marked where it happens; the point net worth would first hit zero (if any) is marked too.

Pension income vs outgoings

HZN-107: both drawdown methods shown together — Method A (flat 4%) and Method B (income-needs) — against total annual expenditure (target living cost + housing + any one-off costs that year). Run-out points for each method are marked.

Figures shown are gross — no tax modelling is applied to pension drawdown (HZN-206), so actual take-home income in retirement will be lower once Income Tax on pension withdrawals is accounted for.

DC pension pot value

Combined value of every Defined Contribution pot, tracked under both drawdown methods — now shown across the full accumulation-then-drawdown lifecycle (HZN-202 fixed the pre-retirement value, which used to read £0).

Death & survivor assumptions

HZN-109: both alive / Pete dies first / Lex dies first — the second death is never modelled, since only one person remains to support after the first. Defined Benefit pensions (Strathclyde LGPS etc.) continue to the surviving spouse at a survivor percentage (set per pension in Setup → Pensions); State Pension for the deceased stops; DC pots pass to the survivor and simply keep being drawn from the combined total.

Exact Strathclyde LGPS survivor percentage and any lump-sum death grant should be confirmed from the SPFO member portal statement — the default of 50% here is a placeholder (open data item per the brief). DC death-benefit tax treatment depends on age at death, provider and current legislation and isn't modelled beyond "the combined pot continues".

Survivability threshold stress test (HZN-110)

For each phase, death is simulated at every candidate age of the dying person to find the earliest age at which the surviving household remains fully funded (net worth never hits zero) all the way to the end of the plan. Uses the scenario and drawdown method selected on the Overview tab.

One-off costs

Timed, lumpy costs a monthly figure can't capture — a boiler, a car, a wedding. Each is entered in today's money and draws from a pot you nominate (Emergency, Growth, or Pension) at the age it happens, or repeats every N years. "Owned only" costs (repairs, renovations) only apply once a home is actually owned.

Simplifications: purchase costs are a flat estimate rather than banded stamp duty; growth is annual not monthly; no tax, ISA/pension allowance, or income-tax modelling. Every figure is calculated in nominal (future) pounds internally — that's the only way compounding stays correct — and only converted to today's money at display time when "Real" is selected on the Overview tab. Enough to compare shapes of futures, not a substitute for a mortgage advisor, pensions administrator, or independent financial advice.

Horizon is a scenario-comparison tool for household planning conversations, not financial advice. Projections depend entirely on the assumptions you set above and are illustrative, not a forecast or a recommendation to buy, rent, or invest in any particular way. Pension figures are estimates built from what you enter — check accrued defined benefit amounts against your latest annual benefit statement, and your own State Pension forecast at gov.uk, rather than relying on this app's defaults.
